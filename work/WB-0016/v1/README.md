# Hilbert's Nullstellensatz is in AM from a zero-free half-plane of Dedekind zeta functions, after Koiran

WB-0016, version 1: [hexagon:2611.01018](https://hexagonmath.org/2611.01018), deposited 25 November 2026.

In 1996 Koiran proved, assuming the generalized Riemann hypothesis (GRH), that deciding whether a system of polynomial equations with integer coefficients has a complex solution (Hilbert's Nullstellensatz, HN) is in the Arthur-Merlin class AM, hence in the second level of the polynomial hierarchy. GRH enters his proof through one estimate, a count of the primes modulo which an irreducible integer polynomial has a root. We show that this estimate, with exponent $7/8+\varepsilon$ in place of $1/2$, follows in two lines from the effective Chebotarev theorem in B. Hu's preprint on the zero-free half-plane $\Re s>7/8$, and that Koiran's proof then runs with primes of an explicit, polynomial bit length. Any zero-free half-plane $\Re s>\theta$, $\theta<1$, common to all Dedekind zeta functions suffices, for instance $\Re s>31/32$ of Cureton and Thorner: the estimate is then Frei, Sofos and Widmer's effective Chebotarev theorem, as Baldwin and Sivek used it for one polynomial in work on 3-manifold recognition, and the step to AM is Proposition 17.2 of our paper hexagon:2611.00987. So HN is in AM granted Hu's theorem or that of Cureton and Thorner. Both are unrefereed preprints of October 2026; the second is formalized in Lean according to its authors. Through published reductions that use GRH only via Koiran's theorem, the dimension problem and the parametric Nullstellensatz are then in AM, vanishing of Schubert coefficients is in AM $\cap$ coAM in types $A$-$D$, so it is not NP-hard unless the polynomial hierarchy collapses to its second level, and homogeneous systems of parameters are constructible in $\mathrm{FP}^{\Sigma_2^{\mathrm P}}$. Andrews, Garg and Schost placed HN in the counting hierarchy with no hypothesis; AM is the smaller class.
